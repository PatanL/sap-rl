# Goal: keep making the Super Auto Pets bot stronger

Written 2026-11-23 and corrected by the user the same day. The user changes the goal; the agent only keeps the status section at the bottom current.

## The goal

A Turtle Pack 1v1 agent that keeps getting stronger, aiming to beat Meshy's bot and strong human players. There is no end condition for now: nobody we can test against is available, so the job is continuous, measured improvement. "Superhuman" stays the direction, and is not claimed without outside evidence.

What "stronger" means until outside opponents exist: a new checkpoint beats the previous champion on the frozen engine, seat-swapped on paired seeds, with the interval clear of 50%, confirmed a second time on unused seeds. Also track it against a fixed panel of older checkpoints so that progress is not just cycling.

Start from checkpoint 25,772. Do not retrain from a fresh smaller network unless continuing clearly fails.

Human games may be used in two ways, both chosen by the user:
- to check the rules and to measure the bot;
- to learn what strong players do (Max Wu, monkey, on SAP Library) and to put the bot into positions where it can try those lines itself. The bot still chooses its own actions and learns from results; it is not trained to copy human moves.

## Tracks, run in parallel

**Train: make it fast first (user, 2026-11-23).** Before any long run, optimise training speed: the weight update, collection that keeps going during an update, the balance between them, and then the engine. Short runs are for checking correctness and measuring speed. When a long run does start: continue 25,772 with the wide batched collector on the best engine available, the historical learning rates unless a comparison says otherwise, and frozen opponents in the pool; score every checkpoint automatically against the frozen champion and the panel; when the engine is corrected, carry the weights onto the new engine rather than restarting, and re-score the champion on it.

**Engine truth.** Keep closing the gap to the real game. Truth sources, in order: real replays from the SAP Library API; the real client replaying an injected battle; SAP-Calculator only as a hint. Every rule change is tested on all development fights, must break none, and is scored once on replays it was not developed on. Targets: at most 1% of battles not reproduced and under 0.1% wrong winner on untouched replays; at least 99.5% of replayable shop turns.

**Engine speed.** Rust battle engine matching the Python reference exactly, seed for seed, on the golden traces; then the shop; then bindings. Success is a measured speed-up of full self-play collection. Target set by the user on 2026-11-23: about 1,000 games played per second, loosely, without tuning toward shorter games; everything, the shop included, goes to Rust.

**New lines.** Ways to get the bot to discover and use multi-turn lines:
- exact end-of-turn positioning by simulation, opponents trained to exploit the champion, randomised pet pools, endgame rollouts;
- the user's idea, optional: a curriculum of starting positions. Seed some games from positions where a line is available and let the bot play on from there. Positions the user listed:
  1. Elephant on board, with Blowfish or Camel in the shop.
  2. Mammoth with Mushroom on board, Tiger in the shop (turn 11 and later).
  3. Flamingo on board with Sleeping Pill in the shop (turns 3 to 6); Turtle on board with Pill in the shop (turns 5 to 10).
  4. Snail and deliberate tempo trades: at six hearts on turn 2 a loss is restored, so it can activate Snail for free; more generally, do not spend heavily on a fight already won or lost.
  5. Horse or Cricket on board with the other in the shop (turn 2); a level-2 Horse with Sheep as the turn-3 tier-up; a level-2 Horse with Dog and Sheep as two tier-ups. Later, Sheep, Deer, Whale, Rooster and Fly feeding Turkey or Shark, minding board space and summon order.
  Validate a curriculum three ways: how often the bot takes the line's key action in held-out versions of those positions; how often the line shows up in its ordinary games from turn 1; and, the one that counts, whether it beats the champion in ordinary games.
- Analyse Max Wu's and monkey's games from SAP Library to see which lines they play and when, as a source of positions and as a yardstick.
