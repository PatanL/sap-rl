## Frame tool

For each captured battle the tool saves the exact input bytes, local response hash, full-size screenshots and elapsed timestamps. Dev and constructed captures additionally request a movie of the browser’s own canvas. The round reader locates paired red damage numerals at the two front positions, extracts the clearest preceding board and names it by stage. The frame before impact one represents the start-of-battle result; the frame before impact two represents the result of attack one. The final frame comes after the watch request. Missing, repeated or delayed damage animations can make a round boundary uncertain. The original frame sequence or movie is retained for adjudication.

The number reader identifies attack/HP badges and classifies rendered digits using the September glyph templates. Sprite labels use captured **starting** formations and visual anchors; unknown labels, ambiguous perks and weak digit matches remain null. The twenty terminal comparisons above use visual readings, independently of the automated per-round recognizer. A clear frame remains evidence even where the machine readout is incomplete. [Capture](battle_viewer.py) · [High-frequency movie boundaries](video_read.py) · [Board reader](rendered_boards.py)

With the authorized guest in a resumable Arena shop that exposes the rewatch arrow, a single new input can be captured by running the following from this directory. Choose a fresh label for a changed input. The launcher never clicks the Arena Start button. It needs the ordinary client session or another confirmed viewer entry point to initiate battle/get.

```bash
./run_one.sh /absolute/path/battle.json my-battle
```

**How to read the client's numbers.** Two things were learned about the display and are used throughout.

- The client shows health below zero for a pet that has just died, so overkill is readable.
- **Label lag.** When a pet is hit or buffed twice in quick succession (a Tiger repeat, two Wolverines, two Mammoth buffs), the label shows the first change only, until that pet's next change. In test f2x the Tiger shows 18 after two 2-damage blasts and 12 after the next 4-damage hit, so it was at 16. A terminal frame can therefore be one change stale; where it disagrees with the archived ResultingBoard, the archive is right. This explains session 2's one mismatch (fight 6b2e1d99: client 8/9, archive 8/8). `pair3.py` reports a strict first difference and one that tolerates a stale label; every rule below rests on a difference that survives the tolerant reading or on arithmetic at the pet's next change.
